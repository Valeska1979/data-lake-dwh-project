\section{Project Idea and Use Case}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Topic Introduction}
\label{sec:topic_introduction}

Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East continue to play a critical role in shaping global financial markets. In the last months, the impact of the Iran conflict on oil prices and stock markets has become a persistent theme in international news coverage. Recent developments, including the escalation of the Iran war in March and April 2026, have been associated with sharp reactions in crude oil markets, renewed volatility in equity markets, and major concerns about supply disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz. Previous research has shown that geopolitical risks and disruptions in oil-producing regions can have significant effects on energy markets and oil prices (Caldara \& Iacoviello, 2022; Kilian, 2009).

Despite these visible market reactions, the relationship between geopolitical events and financial market dynamics remains complex and difficult to quantify. While it is generally assumed that conflicts and escalations lead to increases in oil prices, the extent to which equity markets, particularly major oil and energy companies, respond in a similar or divergent way is less clear. Furthermore, different types of geopolitical events may affect markets in different ways depending on their severity, location, and implications for global energy supply. Previous research suggests that geopolitical risk and political uncertainty can have significant effects on both commodity and financial markets, although the magnitude and persistence of these effects vary across events and market segments (Caldara \& Iacoviello, 2022; Kilian, 2009; Baker et al., 2016).

This project addresses this challenge by designing and implementing a cloud-based data pipeline to analyze the impact of Middle Eastern geopolitical events on both oil prices and energy sector stocks. The pipeline integrates multiple data sources, including oil price data (Brent and WTI), stock price data of major oil and energy companies, real-time news data with sentiment information, and AI-generated classifications of geopolitical events. Using a data lake and data warehouse architecture, the project enables the structured collection, storage, and analysis of heterogeneous data in a scalable and reproducible way.

The relevance of this topic lies in both its economic importance and its technical implications. From a business perspective, understanding how geopolitical events influence oil prices and related stock markets is highly valuable for commodity traders, financial analysts, and geopolitical risk experts. From a technical perspective, the project demonstrates how modern cloud technologies can be used to build an end-to-end data pipeline that supports data ingestion, enrichment, and analysis across multiple data domains. By combining real-world events with structured market data, the project provides a practical framework for analyzing complex relationships in dynamic market environments.

\subsection{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

This project is motivated by the strong and ongoing influence of geopolitical developments in the Middle East on global financial markets. Events involving Iran, such as sanctions, military escalations, uncertainty and rapidly changing conditions regarding the status of the Strait of Hormuz, or diplomatic negotiations, are regularly associated with sudden changes not only in oil prices but also in the stock prices of major oil and energy companies. Previous research has shown that political uncertainty can significantly affect asset prices and risk premia in financial markets (Pastor \& Veronesi, 2013). In addition, disruptions in oil supply and energy markets have long been recognized as important drivers of economic and financial-market fluctuations (Hamilton, 2003). Given the economic importance of both commodity markets and equity markets, understanding these relationships is of high relevance for market participants, analysts, and even consumers, as fluctuations in oil prices are directly reflected in retail fuel prices.

In addition to its economic significance, the topic provides a suitable use case for applying concepts from the Data Lakes and Data Warehouses module. The integration of heterogeneous data sources, including oil price data, stock market data, and AI-generated classifications of geopolitical events, enables the analysis of complex relationships between geopolitical events and different segments of financial markets. Such a setup reflects real-world analytical challenges, where data is distributed across multiple sources, formats, and domains.

Furthermore, the project enables the practical implementation of a cloud-based data pipeline using modern technologies. By combining data ingestion, storage, transformation, and analysis within a unified architecture, it is demonstrated how scalable and automated systems can be built to support data-driven insights across multiple asset classes. This makes the topic not only relevant from a business perspective but also valuable from a technical and educational standpoint. 

\subsection{Problem Definition and Goals}
\label{sec:problem_goals}

\subsubsection{Business and Research Questions}
\label{sec:research_questions}

The data lake implementation answers the following research questions:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Q\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*]
  \item How do Brent and WTI oil prices behave during Iran-region
        conflict periods, and do they move together or diverge?
  \item Do individual oil-sector stocks react differently to conflict
        periods, and which stocks consistently gain or lose?
  \item Which specific conflict events had the largest observable
        impact on oil prices and stock returns?
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Methodology.}
The three research questions are answered through an event-study
design, a widely used approach for analysing the impact of specific
events on financial-market outcomes (MacKinlay, 1997; Campbell et al.,
1997). The methodology aligns three independent data sources on a
shared trading calendar. Stock-price data is sourced from Yahoo Finance
via the \texttt{yfinance} Python library. The project proposal
originally specified Alpha Vantage's
\texttt{TIME\_SERIES\_DAILY\_ADJUSTED} endpoint, but that endpoint was
moved to a premium tier shortly after the project began, prompting the
substitution to \texttt{yfinance}. Oil-price series are pulled from the
U.S. Energy Information Administration's open API. Conflict event data
is generated through a Gemini-based ingestion pipeline using Google
Search grounding and structured LLM classification. The pipeline
extracts and classifies Iran-region geopolitical events into a
five-category taxonomy. A conformed \texttt{dim\_date} table joins the
three domains on shared trading days, which is the precondition for
aligning oil prices and individual stock returns with the conflict
periods identified by the pipeline.

\subsubsection{Business Intelligence Questions}
\label{sec:bi_questions}

The data warehouse implementation answers the following business
intelligence questions:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{BI\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*]
  \item How much do Brent and WTI prices move during conflict periods,
        and does the magnitude differ between event categories?
  \item Which individual stocks show the strongest and most consistent
        reaction to conflict periods?
  \item During which specific conflict periods were oil-price and
        stock-return movements largest?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Data Lake}
\label{sec:data_lake}

\subsection{Data Sources}
\label{sec:data_sources}

The project uses three complementary dynamic data sources.

\paragraph{Stock-price data (yfinance).}
Daily Open, High, Low, Close, and Volume (OHLCV) observations, together
with split- and dividend-adjusted closing prices, are retrieved using the
open-source Python library \texttt{yfinance}, which provides programmatic
access to Yahoo Finance's historical market data.

The instrument universe comprises fifteen tickers selected to represent
the major segments of the global oil and energy sector. These include
integrated oil majors (XOM, CVX, SHEL), upstream exploration and
production companies (COP, OXY, DVN), downstream refiners (VLO, MPC),
oilfield service providers (SLB, HAL, BKR), tanker shipping (FRO), and
sector benchmarks (XLE, USO, SPY).

The original project specification proposed the use of Alpha Vantage's
\texttt{TIME\_SERIES\_DAILY\_ADJUSTED} endpoint. However, this approach
was abandoned after the endpoint was moved to a premium tier,
making the original free-tier assumption no longer viable.

A key limitation of \texttt{yfinance} is that it acts as an unofficial
client for an undocumented Yahoo Finance backend and therefore provides
no formal guarantees regarding long-term availability or schema stability.
This limitation, together with a contingency migration path to an official
paid data provider, is discussed in Section~8.

\paragraph{Oil-price data (EIA API).}
Oil price data is sourced from the U.S. Energy Information Administration
(EIA) API, which provides daily observations for the Brent and WTI
benchmarks in a structured and machine-readable format. These benchmarks are widely recognised indicators of global oil market
developments and are particularly relevant for analysing the impact of
geopolitical events on energy markets (Hamilton, 2003; Kilian, 2009).

As the project aims to investigate potential relationships between Middle
East conflicts and oil price movements, the data directly supports the
use case by providing reliable and timely market information.
Furthermore, the API enables automated data retrieval and seamless
integration into a cloud-based data lake architecture, supporting
scalable ingestion, storage, transformation, and downstream analytics.

A limitation of the dataset is that it provides daily observations only.
Consequently, intraday market reactions and short-term price volatility
cannot be captured. The analysis therefore focuses on daily price
movements rather than immediate market responses to geopolitical events.

\paragraph{Classified events (Gemini API).}
Geopolitical conflict-event data is sourced through Gemini 2.5 Flash with
Google Search grounding. The data source is used to identify and classify
Iran-region geopolitical events that may influence oil prices and energy
markets. For each event, the pipeline extracts structured attributes
including the event date, category, severity, actors, location, short
summary, description, date confidence, and source hint. These attributes
provide the contextual information required to analyse potential
relationships between conflict events, oil-price movements, and stock
market reactions.

The original design used the World News API for article retrieval and
Gemini only for event classification. However, implementation testing
showed that free-tier news APIs, including World News API and comparable
alternatives, did not provide sufficient historical coverage for the
2025--2026 event-study window. The final design therefore relies on
Gemini 2.5 Flash with Google Search grounding, which preserves the
dynamic nature of the source while providing access to the historical
event coverage required by the research questions.

A limitation of this approach is that event identification,
classification, and severity assessment depend on a large language model.
As a result, relevant events may be missed and classifications may not
always be fully consistent. To mitigate this risk, the pipeline applies
a fixed event taxonomy, strict JSON validation, and confidence-based
filtering before events are incorporated into downstream analyses.

\paragraph{Data Catalog.}
All datasets across the bronze, silver, and gold zones are registered in
the AWS Glue Data Catalog under the database
\texttt{dwl\_datapowerchords}. The catalog serves as a central metadata
repository and stores information about dataset schemas, storage
locations, file formats, and partition structures.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/glue_catalog.png}
  \caption[Glue Data Catalog.]{AWS Glue Data Catalog view showing registered datasets across the bronze, silver, and gold zones.}
  \label{fig:glue}
\end{figure}

The catalog is used by AWS Athena for data discovery and querying.
To support governance and ownership, datasets are tagged with metadata
attributes including \texttt{project}, \texttt{owner}, and
\texttt{domain}, enabling datasets to be organised by project,
responsible team member, and business domain.

\paragraph{Schema and Versioning Strategy.}
All silver-zone datasets are written using predefined PyArrow schemas to
ensure consistent data structures and data types across ingestion runs.
Schema evolution follows an additive-only approach. New columns are
accepted and incorporated into the schema, while removed or renamed
columns are treated as breaking changes and trigger transformation
failures. Such failures are reported through Amazon SNS notifications,
allowing schema changes in upstream data sources to be detected and
addressed promptly.

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:data_lake_architecture}

The data lake is implemented on AWS in the \texttt{us-east-1} region.
Figure~1 shows the high-level architecture. Three independent dynamic
data sources feed a daily ingestion layer based on EventBridge and AWS
Lambda. The ingestion functions write the raw API responses to the S3
bronze zone. Transform Lambdas then standardise and convert the data
into curated silver datasets and analytics-ready gold datasets in
Parquet format. All zones are registered in the AWS Glue Data Catalog.

The gold zone serves two downstream paths. First, it can be queried
directly through Athena, which provides a fallback and interim analysis
path before warehouse loading. Second, the gold datasets are loaded into
Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL, which serves as the primary warehouse layer
for Tableau dashboards and cross-domain analytical queries.

\paragraph{Storage Layers and Zones.}
Storage Layers and Zones. The data lake follows a layered architecture
that separates raw, curated, and analytics-ready data (standard bronze--silver--gold architecture, implemented using three Amazon S3 buckets) to improve
governance, traceability, and analytical usability (Inmon et al.,
2016). Each layer serves a distinct
purpose within the data pipeline:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bronze (raw)}
        (\texttt{dwl-datapowerchords-raw}): stores the original API
        responses in JSON format. This layer acts as the system's
        source of truth and preserves the data in its original form.

  \item \textbf{Silver (curated)}
        (\texttt{dwl-datapowerchords-curated}): contains cleaned,
        standardised, and validated datasets in Parquet format. The
        silver layer provides a consistent structure for downstream
        processing and integration across domains.

  \item \textbf{Gold (analytics-ready)}
        (\texttt{dwl-datapowerchords-gold}): stores aggregated and
        business-ready datasets prepared for analytics, reporting, and
        loading into the data warehouse.
\end{itemize}

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{figures/s3buckets.png}
  \caption[Overview of S3 buckets.] {Overview of S3 buckets used for the bronze, silver, and gold layers of the data lake.}
  \label{fig:s3buckets}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{File Formats.}
JSON is used in the bronze layer to preserve the complete API responses
in their original format. For the silver and gold layers, Parquet with
Snappy compression is used to provide efficient storage and query
performance. As most analytical queries involve filtering and
aggregating data across date ranges, the columnar structure of Parquet
is well suited to the project's reporting and analytics requirements.

\paragraph{Streaming vs Batch.}
The project follows a batch-processing approach. Data ingestion is
scheduled once per day using Amazon EventBridge, with subsequent
transformation and loading steps executed automatically through the
pipeline. This frequency is sufficient for the daily oil-price,
stock-market, and geopolitical event data used in the analysis. A
streaming architecture was considered unnecessary, as the business
questions focus on daily market movements rather than real-time event
processing.

\paragraph{Interoperability with the Data Warehouse.}
The gold layer acts as the integration point between the data lake and
the data warehouse. Analytics-ready datasets stored in the gold zone
are loaded into Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL, where they are transformed
into the dimensional models and star schemas used for reporting and
analysis (Kimball \& Ross, 2013).

As illustrated in Figure~1, the architecture supports two analytical
access paths. Data can be queried directly from the data lake through
Athena, or through the PostgreSQL data warehouse after the loading
process has been completed. This dual approach provides flexibility for
both exploratory analysis and dashboard-driven reporting.

\section{Data Ingestion}
\label{sec:data_ingestion}

All three data sources are accessed through dynamic APIs over HTTPS.
Ingestion follows a common pattern: an EventBridge rule triggers a
domain-specific Lambda function, which retrieves the required API
credentials from AWS Secrets Manager, issues the relevant API requests,
and stores the unmodified JSON responses in the bronze layer of the
data lake. Basic error handling is implemented through logging, retry
mechanisms where appropriate, and automated SNS alerts to notify the
team of ingestion failures.

\paragraph{API Details.}
All three sources are accessed through HTTPS-based API requests and are
ingested by dedicated AWS Lambda functions. The EIA API and Gemini API
require authentication through API keys stored in AWS Secrets Manager,
whereas yfinance accesses Yahoo Finance's public quotation service
without authentication. The low request volume of the project remains
well below the documented or observed rate limits of all sources.
Pagination is not required, as the requested data volumes are returned
within single responses or bounded query windows. Retrieved data is
stored in the bronze layer as raw JSON before being transformed into
curated Parquet datasets in the silver and gold layers.

\paragraph{Source Selection and Use-Case Support.}
The selected sources directly support the project's business questions.
yfinance provides historical stock-price data for major oil companies,
energy-sector benchmarks, and market-control instruments, enabling the
analysis of stock-market reactions to geopolitical events. The EIA API
provides daily Brent and WTI benchmark prices, which serve as key
indicators of global oil-market developments. Gemini 2.5 Flash with
Google Search grounding supplies structured information about
Iran-related geopolitical conflict events, including event categories,
severity levels, locations, and descriptions. Together, the three
sources provide the market, commodity, and geopolitical dimensions
required to investigate the relationship between Middle East conflict
events, oil prices, and energy-sector stock performance.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Each source has limitations that must be considered when interpreting
the results. The yfinance library relies on an unofficial Yahoo Finance
backend and therefore offers no formal service-level guarantees. The
EIA API provides daily observations only and cannot capture intraday
market reactions. The Gemini-based event source introduces model risk,
as event identification and classification depend on a generative AI
model and may therefore contain omissions or classification errors.
These limitations are mitigated through validation procedures, data
persistence in the bronze layer, and the use of confidence and
severity-based filtering in downstream processing.

\subsection{Pipeline Design}
\label{sec:pipeline_design}

All three domains use the same orchestration pattern: EventBridge
rules trigger the ingest, transform, and load Lambda functions

\subsection{Data Capture \& Handling}
\label{sec:data_capture}

\paragraph{Schema Evolution.}
Predefined schemas are used throughout the data pipeline to ensure
consistent data structures across ingestion and transformation stages.
Schema-related issues are detected during processing and reported
through the monitoring and alerting mechanisms of the platform.

\paragraph{Retries and Error Handling.}
The pipeline includes error-handling mechanisms to improve operational
reliability. Execution logs are recorded in Amazon CloudWatch,
providing visibility into pipeline activity and supporting
troubleshooting. When failures occur, automated SNS notifications alert
the team and enable timely investigation and recovery.

\paragraph{Duplicates and Late-Arriving Data.}
Warehouse load operations use
\texttt{INSERT ... ON CONFLICT ... DO UPDATE} statements based on the
natural key of each fact table. This approach makes the loading process
idempotent, prevents duplicate records, and allows corrected or
late-arriving source data to be processed without manual intervention.

\section{Data Storage}
\label{sec:data_storage}

\subsection{Storage Layers and Services}
\label{sec:storage_layers}

The project uses two primary storage technologies: Amazon S3 as the
data lake and Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL as the analytical data
warehouse.

\paragraph{Amazon S3.}
Amazon S3 stores all datasets across the bronze, silver, and gold
layers of the data lake. The bronze layer contains raw API responses,
the silver layer contains cleaned and standardised datasets, and the
gold layer contains analytics-ready data products. S3 was selected
because it provides scalable, cost-effective storage and integrates
seamlessly with AWS services such as Glue and Athena. The separation of storage layers supports the
progressive refinement of data from raw ingestion to analytical
consumption (Inmon et al., 2016).

\paragraph{Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL.}
Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL serves as the analytical data warehouse. Gold
datasets are loaded into dimensional models and star schemas that
support cross-domain analysis and Tableau dashboards. PostgreSQL was
selected because of its mature SQL capabilities, support for
relationships and constraints, and suitability for structured
analytical workloads.

\subsection{Data Formats and Optimisation}
\label{sec:data_formats}

\paragraph{Bronze Zone: JSON.}
JSON is used in the bronze layer to preserve the original API responses
without modification. Storing the raw data in its native format
provides a reliable source of truth and allows data to be reprocessed
if transformation logic changes in the future.

\paragraph{Silver and Gold Zones: Parquet.}
The silver and gold layers use Apache Parquet with Snappy compression.
Parquet was selected because it is a columnar storage format that
supports efficient analytical queries, reduces storage requirements,
and integrates well with AWS Glue, Amazon Athena, and downstream
analytics tools. In addition, Parquet preserves schema information and
data types, which helps ensure consistency across processing stages.

\paragraph{Partitioning Strategy.}
Datasets are partitioned by relevant date attributes to improve query
performance and reduce the amount of data that must be scanned during
analysis. This approach is particularly beneficial for time-series
queries, which represent the dominant access pattern for oil prices,
stock prices, and geopolitical events.

\subsection{Database Design}
\label{sec:database_design}

The data warehouse follows a Kimball-style dimensional modelling
approach (Kimball \& Ross, 2013). Each domain is implemented as a star
schema consisting of one central fact table and one or more dimension
tables. The schemas share the conformed
\texttt{analytics.dim\_date} dimension, a key concept in dimensional
modelling that enables consistent cross-domain analysis of stock
prices, oil prices, and geopolitical events on a common timeline
(Kimball \& Ross, 2013).

\subsubsection{Stocks Schema}
\label{sec:stocks_schema}

The stocks domain consists of the fact table
\texttt{stocks.fact\_prices} and the dimension table
\texttt{stocks.dim\_ticker}. The grain of the fact table is one stock
price observation per ticker and trading day. The primary key is
\texttt{price\_sk}, while the foreign keys \texttt{ticker\_sk} and
\texttt{date\_sk} link the fact table to the ticker and date
dimensions. The \texttt{stocks.dim\_ticker} table implements Slowly Changing
Dimension Type 2 (SCD2) semantics through the attributes
\texttt{valid\_from} and \texttt{valid\_to}, allowing historical
changes to ticker metadata to be tracked over time (Kimball \& Ross,
2013).

\begin{lstlisting}[language=SQL,caption={Stocks fact and dimension tables.},label={lst:stocks_schema}]
CREATE TABLE stocks.fact_prices (
  price_sk BIGSERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
  ticker_sk BIGINT NOT NULL REFERENCES stocks.dim_ticker(ticker_sk),
  date_sk INTEGER NOT NULL REFERENCES analytics.dim_date(date_sk),
  trade_date DATE NOT NULL,
  close NUMERIC(18,6),
  volume BIGINT,
  log_return NUMERIC(18,8),
  UNIQUE (ticker_sk, trade_date)
);

CREATE TABLE stocks.dim_ticker (
  ticker_sk BIGSERIAL PRIMARY KEY,
  ticker TEXT NOT NULL,
  company_name TEXT,
  valid_from TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
  valid_to TIMESTAMPTZ
);
\end{lstlisting}

\label{sec:analytics_schema}

The \texttt{analytics} schema contains the conformed

the stocks, oil, and news domains through the shared date dimension,
enabling unified analysis across all three subject areas.

The mart \texttt{mart\_daily\_signals} provides a daily time series

event-window analysis by comparing market behaviour before and after

different conflict types and stock performance.

The \texttt{dim\_date} table serves as the conformed date dimension for the entire warehouse (Kimball \& Ross, 2013). It contains calendar, trading-day, and holiday
attributes and is referenced by all fact tables and analytical marts
through the surrogate key \texttt{date\_sk}. This shared dimension
ensures consistent temporal analysis across the stocks, oil, and news
domains and forms the foundation for cross-domain reporting and
dashboard visualisations.

\section{Data Transformation}
\label{sec:data_transformation}

The transformation layer converts raw API payloads into
analytics-ready Parquet and finally into the RDS star schema. Three
stages apply across all domains: bronze $\to$ silver (parsing,
normalization, PyArrow schema enforcement), silver $\to$ gold
(grouping, partitioning), and gold $\to$ RDS (idempotent upsert).

\subsection{Transformation Logic}
\label{sec:transformation_logic}

\subsubsection{Stocks}
\label{sec:stocks_transform}

The stocks transformation pipeline converts raw market data into
analytics-ready datasets. During the first stage, the bronze JSON
files are parsed and transformed into a standardised schema. Field
names are normalised, data types are validated, and daily log returns
are calculated as

\[
r_t = \ln\left(\frac{P_t}{P_{t-1}}\right)
\]

where \(P_t\) represents the adjusted closing price
(\texttt{adj\_close}) on day \(t\). For the first observation of a
ticker, no prior price exists and the corresponding
\texttt{log\_return} value is therefore set to \texttt{NULL}.

During the silver transformation stage, the cleaned records are stored
as Parquet files partitioned by ticker, year, and month. This layer
provides a curated representation of the original source data while
preserving the complete historical time series.

In the gold stage, records from all 15 tickers are consolidated into a
common analytical structure and partitioned by trading date. Additional
attributes such as \texttt{ticker\_sk} and \texttt{date\_sk} are
generated to support integration with the warehouse star schema. The
resulting dataset serves as the basis for downstream analytics,
cross-domain joins, and dashboard visualisations.

\subsection{Data Cleaning and Preparation}
\label{sec:data_cleaning}

\paragraph{Missing values.}
Missing values are handled according to the characteristics of each
data source. Non-trading days, such as weekends and public holidays,
are represented by the absence of observations rather than through
interpolation or forward-filling. For conflict-event data, days
without relevant events are explicitly recorded.

\paragraph{Outliers.}
Basic validation checks are applied during the transformation process
to identify implausible values and malformed records. Records failing
validation are excluded from further processing and logged for review.

\paragraph{Duplicates.}
Duplicate records are prevented through idempotent processing and
warehouse upserts using \texttt{INSERT \dots ON CONFLICT \dots DO
UPDATE}, ensuring that repeated processing does not create duplicate
entries.

\paragraph{Standardisation.}
Dates, timestamps, and numerical values are standardised across all
domains. Additional surrogate keys such as \texttt{date\_sk} are
generated to support integration between the stocks, oil, and news
datasets.

\subsection{Data Quality and Validation}
\label{sec:data_quality}

Data quality is assessed across four dimensions: accuracy,
consistency, completeness, and timeliness. Validation checks verify
that records conform to the expected schema, required attributes are
present, and key relationships remain consistent across datasets.

Completeness is monitored through row-count comparisons and loading
statistics, while timeliness is ensured through the scheduled daily
ingestion and transformation processes. Quality checks are executed
after loading into the warehouse, and detected issues trigger SNS
notifications for follow-up.

\paragraph{Monitoring.}
Operational monitoring is implemented through CloudWatch metrics and
logs. Significant deviations in record volumes or processing results
generate alerts and support the early detection of data-quality issues
or upstream source changes.

\subsection{Scalability and Reusability}
\label{sec:scalability}

The transformation pipelines were designed with reusability and
modularity in mind. Each domain follows the same bronze--silver--gold
pattern and uses separate ingestion, transformation, and loading
components. This structure simplifies maintenance and allows
individual pipeline stages to be modified without affecting the
overall architecture.

Common functionality is implemented through shared Python layers,
standardised schemas, and reusable processing patterns across the
different domains. This approach reduces duplication and simplifies
the integration of additional data sources or analytical domains.

The modular architecture also supports future scaling. If data
volumes or processing requirements increase significantly, individual
pipeline components could be migrated from AWS Lambda to more scalable
services such as AWS Glue without fundamental changes to the overall
design.

\section{Data Warehouse}
\label{sec:data_warehouse}

\subsection{Business Goals and Objectives}
\label{sec:business_goals}

The objective of the data warehouse is to support the analysis of
relationships between geopolitical conflict events, oil prices, and
stock-market performance. The warehouse integrates data from all three
domains into a common analytical structure, enabling cross-domain
queries and dashboard-based exploration.

\paragraph{Stakeholders.}
The primary stakeholders are geopolitical risk analysts, energy-market
analysts, financial-market researchers, and investment professionals
interested in understanding how conflict events influence oil prices
and energy-sector equities.

\paragraph{Key Metrics and Reports.}
The warehouse provides daily stock returns, oil prices, conflict-event
counts, event severity measures, and derived cross-domain indicators.
Analytical marts support reporting on event impacts, market reactions,
and relationships between conflict categories and financial-market
movements.

\paragraph{Granularity.}
The warehouse operates at daily granularity. This level was selected
because all three domains can be aligned consistently on a common
daily timeline, while intraday data was outside the scope of the
available data sources and project requirements.

\subsection{Data Warehouse Modelling}

The data warehouse follows a Kimball-style dimensional modelling
approach (Kimball \& Ross, 2013). Each domain is implemented within its
own schema (\texttt{stocks}, \texttt{oil}, and
\texttt{news\_events}), while the shared \texttt{analytics} schema
contains the conformed \texttt{dim\_date} dimension and the
cross-domain analytical marts.

The warehouse integrates stock-market data, oil-price observations,
and geopolitical conflict events into a common analytical structure.
All fact tables are linked through the shared
\texttt{analytics.dim\_date} dimension, enabling consistent
cross-domain analysis and reporting.

    \texttt{analytics}   & \texttt{mart\_conflict\_sessions} & calendar day $\times$ ticker, severity-5 session windows (±3 days), forward-filled oil prices \\
    \texttt{analytics}   & \texttt{mart\_category\_impact}   & top event category $\times$ ticker, aggregated return statistics \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Surrogate Keys.}
Surrogate Keys. Fact and dimension tables use surrogate keys to ensure
stable joins across the warehouse, following common dimensional
modelling practices (Kimball \& Ross, 2013).

\paragraph{Hierarchies.}
Hierarchies. The warehouse supports hierarchical analysis through both
the date and ticker dimensions, a common feature of dimensional models
used for analytical reporting (Kimball \& Ross, 2013).

\paragraph{Domain Models.}
The stocks schema contains a daily price fact table and an SCD Type 2
ticker dimension. The oil schema contains a daily oil-price fact table
covering Brent and WTI benchmarks. The news schema consists of a daily
conflict-event fact table supported by category and severity
dimensions. Together, these schemas provide the foundation for the
cross-domain analytical marts described below.

\subsubsection{Stocks Star Schema Contribution}
\label{sec:dw_stocks}

The stocks domain contributes the
\texttt{stocks.fact\_prices} fact table and the
\texttt{stocks.dim\_ticker} dimension. The fact table stores daily
market observations at the grain of one ticker per trading day and is
linked to both the ticker and date dimensions. The ticker dimension
uses SCD Type 2 semantics to support the historical tracking of
metadata changes.

\subsubsection{Oil Star Schema Contribution}
\label{sec:dw_oil}

The oil domain contributes the \texttt{oil.fact\_prices} fact table.
The grain of the table is one oil benchmark and trading day. The
shared \texttt{date\_sk} key links oil-price observations to the
conformed date dimension, enabling integration with stock-market and
conflict-event data.

\subsubsection{News Events Schema Contribution}
\label{sec:dw_news}

The news domain contributes the
\texttt{news\_events.fact\_conflict\_daily} fact table together with
the dimensions \texttt{dim\_event\_category} and
\texttt{dim\_severity}. The fact table stores daily aggregated
conflict-event measures, while the dimensions provide descriptive
business context for event categories and severity levels.

\subsubsection{Analytics Mart Contribution}
\label{sec:dw_analytics_marts}

Three analytical marts support the Tableau dashboards. The mart
\texttt{mart\_daily\_signals} combines stock, oil, and conflict-event
data at a common daily grain. The mart
\texttt{mart\_conflict\_sessions} supports event-window analysis
around major conflict events, while
\texttt{mart\_category\_impact} provides aggregated statistics for
evaluating the impact of different conflict categories on stock
performance.

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:dw_architecture}

The data warehouse is implemented on a shared Amazon RDS PostgreSQL
instance and integrates the stocks, oil, and news domains into a
single analytical environment. Data is loaded from the gold layer of
the data lake into dimensional warehouse schemas and analytical marts.
The resulting warehouse serves as the primary source for Tableau
dashboards and cross-domain analysis.

\subsection{Data Preparation}
\label{sec:dw_data_prep}

The selected data sources directly support the project's research
questions. Stock-market data provides information on energy-sector
equity performance, oil-price data captures developments in the
underlying commodity market, and conflict-event data provides the
geopolitical context required to analyse potential market reactions.

\paragraph{Extraction Strategy.}
Data is extracted from the gold layer of the data lake, which contains
the final curated datasets for each domain. Dedicated load processes
read the gold Parquet files and transfer the records into the
warehouse tables.

\paragraph{Transformation Rules.}
Only limited transformations are required during warehouse loading.
The data has already been cleaned and standardised in the data lake.
The loading process therefore focuses on surrogate-key generation,
date-dimension integration, data-type validation, and the mapping of
records into the warehouse star schema.

\paragraph{Loading Strategy.}
The warehouse uses an incremental loading approach. New or updated
records are loaded from the most recent gold partitions and merged
into the warehouse using \texttt{INSERT \dots ON CONFLICT \dots DO
UPDATE}. This approach avoids full reloads while preserving
idempotent processing and supporting historical reprocessing when
required.

\section{Data Visualization}

\subsection{Visualization Purpose}
\label{sec:viz_purpose}

The visualization layer is designed to make the relationships between
Iran-region conflict events, oil-price movements, and energy-sector
stock returns accessible through an interactive dashboard. The main
problem is not only to display these datasets side by side, but to
enable users to select specific conflict periods and immediately
observe corresponding changes in oil prices and stock returns.

The target audience consists of geopolitical risk analysts,
energy-market analysts, financial-market researchers, and investment
professionals interested in the market effects of geopolitical risk.

The dashboard supports decisions and analytical assessments related to
oil-price behaviour during conflict periods (Q1, BI1), differences in
individual stock reactions across the oil-sector universe (Q2, BI2),
and the identification of conflict events or periods with the largest
observable market impact (Q3, BI3).

\subsection{Theoretical Model}
\label{sec:theoretical_model}

The dashboard design follows Shneiderman's visual information-seeking
mantra: \emph{overview first, zoom and filter, then details on
demand}. This framework is widely used in information visualisation
and supports the exploration of complex datasets through progressive
levels of detail (Shneiderman, 1996).

The dashboard therefore provides an initial overview of conflict
periods, oil-price developments, and stock-market performance. Users
can then zoom into specific time periods through interactive
selection and filtering mechanisms. Additional information is provided
through tooltips and detailed event views, enabling users to
investigate individual conflict events and their associated market
reactions.

\subsection{Data Warehouse Visualization}
\label{sec:dw_visualization}

The Tableau workbook is implemented as a single integrated dashboard
designed to answer BI1--BI3 through interactive exploration. The
dashboard combines oil-price data, conflict-event information, and
stock-market performance within a unified analytical view.

A Brent price timeline spanning the full analysis window occupies the
top of the dashboard, with conflict sessions highlighted in red. This
view supports BI1 by allowing users to examine oil-price movements
during different conflict periods. A drag-to-select action on the
timeline updates three linked detail panels: a table of classified
events and severity scores, a Brent and WTI comparison chart, and a
stock-return chart covering all fifteen equities in the dataset.

The event panel supports BI3 by identifying the specific events that
occurred during a selected conflict period, while the stock-return
panel supports BI2 by highlighting differences in stock reactions.
Interactive filtering enables direct comparison between individual
stocks and conflict periods.

\subsection{Answer to Research and Business Questions}
\label{sec:bq_answers}

\subsubsection{Q1/BI1: Oil price behaviour during conflict periods}
\label{sec:answer_q1}

The Brent price timeline in Figure~\ref{fig:dash_overview} shows that
conflict sessions, highlighted in red, coincide with visible
deviations in the Brent price trend. Zooming into individual sessions
(Figure~\ref{fig:dash_session}) confirms that oil prices react within
the session window, with the magnitude varying across episodes
depending on the nature and severity of the triggering events.

Comparing the Brent and WTI series in the oil-detail panel reveals
that the two benchmarks move closely in tandem during conflict
periods, with no persistent divergence between them. Both benchmarks
typically rise during major escalation periods and follow similar
short-term trends throughout the selected conflict windows. 
The strongest oil-price movements are generally associated with
events classified as \texttt{military\_conflict} and
\texttt{security\_incidents}, whereas lower-severity diplomatic and
sanctions-related events tend to coincide with smaller price changes. 

This is
consistent with their shared exposure to Middle East supply-risk
sentiment despite their different delivery locations and market
structures.

\subsubsection{Q2/BI2: Individual stock divergence during conflict periods}
\label{sec:answer_q2}

A key finding of the dashboard is the substantial divergence in stock
reactions during conflict periods. As shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:dash_stocks}, the fifteen stocks do not move as a
uniform group but instead exhibit a wide range of positive, neutral,
and occasionally negative returns within the same conflict session.

The dashboard consistently shows meaningful variation in the
magnitude of stock returns across the analysed universe. While some
stocks experience strong positive returns during a selected conflict
window, others react only weakly or even negatively. The results
therefore suggest that geopolitical conflict events do not affect all
energy-sector equities equally and that company-specific factors play
an important role in determining market reactions.

\subsubsection{Q3/BI3: Most impactful individual conflict events}
\label{sec:answer_q3}

The top-events panel (Figure~\ref{fig:dash_events}) identifies the
specific classified events that occurred within each selected conflict
period together with their associated severity scores. By linking the
event table to the oil-price and stock-performance panels, the
dashboard enables direct comparison between geopolitical events and
subsequent market movements.

Across the analysed period, the largest oil-price and stock-market
movements are associated with high-severity events involving military
escalation, direct attacks, or disruptions to shipping routes in and
around the Strait of Hormuz. Events classified as
\texttt{military\_conflict} and \texttt{security\_incidents}
frequently coincide with the strongest market reactions, whereas
lower-severity diplomatic or sanctions-related events are generally
associated with more limited price movements.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

\subsection{Discussion of the Solution}
\label{sec:discussion}

The project successfully combines data-lake and data-warehouse
concepts within a single analytical platform. The bronze, silver, and
gold architecture provides a clear separation between raw, curated,
and analytics-ready data, while the dimensional warehouse model
enables efficient cross-domain analysis across the stocks, oil, and
news domains.

A key advantage of the chosen architecture is its use of managed AWS
services, including Amazon S3, AWS Lambda, AWS Glue, Amazon Athena,
and Amazon RDS. This reduced operational overhead and allowed the
team to focus on data integration, transformation, and analytical
processing rather than infrastructure management. The combination of a
data lake and a relational warehouse also proved beneficial. Athena
enabled direct exploration of gold-layer Parquet data, while
PostgreSQL provided a structured analytical environment with foreign
keys, dimensional modelling, and fast dashboard queries.

Several trade-offs were identified during implementation. The project
uses a public RDS endpoint and manual AWS Console configuration
instead of a fully automated Infrastructure-as-Code approach. While
this simplified development within the limited project timeframe, a
production deployment would benefit from stronger network isolation
and automated provisioning. In addition, the news pipeline relies on
Gemini-based event discovery and classification. This approach
provided substantially richer historical coverage than the available
free news APIs, but it also introduces model risk because event
classification and severity assessment are generated by an AI model
rather than obtained from a deterministic source.

\subsection{Project Outcomes}
\label{sec:project_outcomes}

The project achieved its primary objective of designing and
implementing an integrated data lake and data warehouse capable of
combining geopolitical conflict events, crude-oil prices, and
energy-sector stock-market data within a single analytical platform.

The resulting solution successfully answered the proposed research
and business intelligence questions. The analysis showed that Brent
and WTI prices generally move together during conflict periods and
that the strongest oil-price reactions are associated with
high-severity military conflicts and security incidents. The stock
analysis revealed substantial variation in how individual energy
stocks react to the same conflict events, indicating that the sector
does not behave as a homogeneous group. Furthermore, the dashboard
enabled the identification of specific conflict periods and events
associated with the largest observable market movements.

From a technical perspective, the project delivered a complete
end-to-end data platform consisting of automated ingestion,
transformation, warehouse loading, dimensional modelling, analytical
marts, and interactive Tableau visualisations. The final solution
demonstrates how data-lake and data-warehouse technologies can be
combined to support both exploratory analysis and business
intelligence reporting.

While the results provide useful insights, they should be interpreted
within the limitations of the available data. The analysis period
covers only a relatively short time window beginning in 2025 and
therefore includes a limited number of major conflict episodes.
Furthermore, the event dataset is based on AI-assisted event
classification, which introduces a degree of uncertainty despite the
validation and filtering mechanisms implemented in the pipeline.

\subsection{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

Potential extensions of the project include:

\begin{itemize}
\item Migration to a VPC-isolated production architecture with
private RDS access and VPC endpoints.

\item Adoption of Infrastructure-as-Code tools such as Terraform to
improve reproducibility and deployment automation.

\item Extension of the analysis period through access to larger
historical news archives and longer stock-price histories.

\item Integration of intraday market data to analyse short-term market
reactions immediately after conflict events.

\item Additional event-validation mechanisms to reduce model risk in
the Gemini-based event-classification process.

\item Expansion of the analytical scope beyond the energy sector to
include broader equity indices, currencies, or commodity markets.
\end{itemize}

import logging
import os
from datetime import datetime, timezone, timedelta
import boto3
import pandas as pd
import yfinance as yf

# Lambda's home filesystem is read-only; redirect yfinance's TzCache + CookieCache
# to /tmp (writable ephemeral storage) to silence the noisy startup warnings.
yf.set_tz_cache_location("/tmp/yf-cache")

logger = logging.getLogger()
logger.setLevel(os.environ.get("LOG_LEVEL", "INFO"))

BUCKET = os.environ["BUCKET"]
BACKFILL_START = os.environ.get("BACKFILL_START", "2025-01-01")
ROUTINE_LOOKBACK_DAYS = int(os.environ.get("ROUTINE_LOOKBACK_DAYS", "7"))

TICKERS = [
    "XOM", "CVX", "SHEL",
    "COP", "OXY", "DVN",
    "VLO", "MPC",
    "SLB", "HAL", "BKR",
    "FRO",
    "XLE", "USO", "SPY",
]

COLUMN_MAP = {
    "Date": "date",
    "Open": "open",
    "High": "high",
    "Low": "low",
    "Close": "close",
    "Adj Close": "adj_close",
    "Volume": "volume",
    "Dividends": "dividends",
    "Stock Splits": "stock_splits",
}

s3 = boto3.client("s3")

def fetch_ticker(symbol, start_date):
    t = yf.Ticker(symbol)
    df = t.history(start=start_date, auto_adjust=False)
    if df.empty:
        raise RuntimeError(f"yfinance returned empty DataFrame for {symbol} (start={start_date})")
    df = df.reset_index()
    if "Datetime" in df.columns and "Date" not in df.columns:
        df = df.rename(columns={"Datetime": "Date"})
    df["Date"] = pd.to_datetime(df["Date"]).dt.strftime("%Y-%m-%d")
    df = df.rename(columns=COLUMN_MAP)
    keep = [v for v in COLUMN_MAP.values() if v in df.columns]
    return df[keep].to_dict(orient="records")


def write_to_s3(symbol, ingest_date, start_date, rows):
    payload = {
        "meta": {
            "symbol": symbol,
            "ingest_date": ingest_date,
            "start_date": start_date,
            "source": "yfinance",
            "row_count": len(rows),
        },
        "rows": rows,
    }
    key = f"stocks/yfinance/symbol={symbol}/ingest_date={ingest_date}/daily.json"
    body = json.dumps(payload).encode("utf-8")
    s3.put_object(Bucket=BUCKET, Key=key, Body=body, ContentType="application/json")
    logger.info("Wrote s3://%s/%s (%d rows, %d bytes)", BUCKET, key, len(rows), len(body))
    return key


def lambda_handler(event, context):
    backfill = bool(event.get("backfill"))
    tickers = event.get("tickers") or TICKERS

    ingest_date = datetime.now(timezone.utc).strftime("%Y-%m-%d")
    if backfill:
        start_date = BACKFILL_START
    else:
        start_date = (datetime.now(timezone.utc).date() - timedelta(days=ROUTINE_LOOKBACK_DAYS)).strftime("%Y-%m-%d")

    written, failed = [], []
    for symbol in tickers:
        try:
            rows = fetch_ticker(symbol, start_date)
            key = write_to_s3(symbol, ingest_date, start_date, rows)
            written.append({"symbol": symbol, "key": key, "rows": len(rows)})
        except Exception as e:
            logger.exception("Failed to ingest %s", symbol)
            failed.append({"symbol": symbol, "error": str(e)})

    result = {
        "ingest_date": ingest_date,
        "start_date": start_date,
        "mode": "backfill" if backfill else "routine",
        "written_count": len(written),
        "failed_count": len(failed),
        "written": written,
        "failed": failed,
    }

    if failed:
        raise RuntimeError(f"{len(failed)} ticker(s) failed: {failed}")

    return result

def _fetch_events_for_week(api_key: str, week_start: date, week_end: date) -> list:
    """Call Gemini 2.5 Flash via REST with Google Search grounding."""
    start_str = week_start.isoformat()
    end_str   = week_end.isoformat()

    prompt = PROMPT_TEMPLATE.format(
        start_date=start_str,
        end_date=end_str,
        max_events=MAX_EVENTS,
    )

    payload = json.dumps({
        "contents": [{"parts": [{"text": prompt}]}],
        "tools":    [{"google_search": {}}],
        "generationConfig": {
            "temperature":     0.0,
            "maxOutputTokens": 16000,
        },
    }).encode("utf-8")

    url = (
        f"https://generativelanguage.googleapis.com/v1beta/models/"
        f"{GEMINI_MODEL}:generateContent?key={api_key}"
    )

    req = urllib.request.Request(
        url,
        data    = payload,
        headers = {"Content-Type": "application/json"},
        method  = "POST",
    )

    logger.info("Calling Gemini for week %s -> %s", start_str, end_str)

    with urllib.request.urlopen(req, timeout=60) as resp:
        result = json.loads(resp.read().decode("utf-8"))

    raw = result["candidates"][0]["content"]["parts"][0]["text"].strip()
    logger.info("Gemini raw response (first 300 chars): %s", raw[:300])

    if raw.startswith("```"):
        raw = raw.split("```")[1]
        if raw.startswith("json"):
            raw = raw[4:]
    raw = raw.strip()

    raw = raw.replace('\u201c', '"').replace('\u201d', '"')
    raw = raw.replace('\u2018', "'").replace('\u2019', "'")

    events = json.loads(raw)
    logger.info("week=%s->%s raw_events=%d", start_str, end_str, len(events))
    return events

def _validate_events(events: list, week_start: str, week_end: str) -> list:
    validated = []
    for e in events:

def _write_to_s3_by_date(events: list, week_start: date, week_end: date) -> list:
    from collections import defaultdict
    by_date = defaultdict(list)
    for e in events:
        by_date[e.get("event_date", week_start.isoformat())].append(e)

    current = week_start
    while current <= week_end:
        date_str = current.isoformat()
        if date_str not in by_date:
            by_date[date_str] = []
        current += timedelta(days=1)

    keys_written = []
    for date_str, day_events in sorted(by_date.items()):
        key  = f"{S3_PREFIX}/event_date={date_str}/events.json"
        body = json.dumps(
            {
                "event_date":  date_str,
                "event_count": len(day_events),
                "events":      day_events,
            },
            ensure_ascii=False,
            indent=2,
        )
        _get_s3_client().put_object(
            Bucket      = BUCKET,
            Key         = key,
            Body        = body.encode("utf-8"),
            ContentType = "application/json",
        )
        logger.info("Wrote %d events to s3://%s/%s", len(day_events), BUCKET, key)
        keys_written.append(key)

    return keys_written

def handler(event: dict, context) -> dict:
  
    api_key = _get_api_key()
    today   = date.today()

    if event.get("backfill"):
        raw_start = event.get("start_date", "2025-01-01")
        raw_end   = event.get("end_date",   today.isoformat())
        start_dt  = date.fromisoformat(raw_start)
        end_dt    = date.fromisoformat(raw_end)
        logger.info("Backfill mode: %s -> %s", raw_start, raw_end)
    else:
        end_dt   = today - timedelta(days=1)
        start_dt = end_dt - timedelta(days=6)
        logger.info("Routine mode: %s -> %s", start_dt, end_dt)

    results = []

    for week_start, week_end in _week_ranges(start_dt, end_dt):
        try:
            events    = _fetch_events_for_week(api_key, week_start, week_end)
            validated = _validate_events(events, week_start.isoformat(), week_end.isoformat())
            keys      = _write_to_s3_by_date(validated, week_start, week_end)

            results.append({
                "week":          week_start.isoformat(),
                "status":        "ok",
                "event_count":   len(validated),
                "files_written": len(keys),
            })

            time.sleep(5)   # avoid Gemini rate limiting

        except urllib.error.HTTPError as exc:
            if exc.code == 429:
                logger.warning("Rate limit hit for week %s - waiting 30s and retrying once", week_start)
                time.sleep(30)
                try:
                    events    = _fetch_events_for_week(api_key, week_start, week_end)
                    validated = _validate_events(events, week_start.isoformat(), week_end.isoformat())
                    keys      = _write_to_s3_by_date(validated, week_start, week_end)
                    results.append({
                        "week":          week_start.isoformat(),
                        "status":        "ok",
                        "event_count":   len(validated),
                        "files_written": len(keys),
                    })
                except Exception as retry_exc:
                    logger.error("Retry failed for week %s - writing empty files: %s", week_start, retry_exc)
                    keys = _write_to_s3_by_date([], week_start, week_end)
                    results.append({"week": week_start.isoformat(), "status": "empty_response", "event_count": 0, "files_written": len(keys)})
            else:
                logger.error("Gemini HTTP error for week %s: %s", week_start, exc)
                results.append({"week": week_start.isoformat(), "status": "http_error", "error": str(exc)})

        except json.JSONDecodeError as exc:
            logger.error("JSON parse error for week %s - Gemini returned non-JSON, storing empty week: %s", week_start, exc)
            keys = _write_to_s3_by_date([], week_start, week_end)
            results.append({
                "week":          week_start.isoformat(),
                "status":        "empty_response",
                "event_count":   0,

    logger.info("Done. %d/%d weeks succeeded.", ok_count, len(results))

    _alert_failures(results, event.get("backfill", False))

        "partitions_attempted": len(targets),

        "partitions_missing": len(partitions_missing),

CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW analytics.mart_conflict_sessions
AS WITH sev5_dates AS (
         SELECT fact_conflict_daily.event_date
           FROM news_events.fact_conflict_daily
          WHERE fact_conflict_daily.max_severity = 5
        ), sev5_gaps AS (
         SELECT sev5_dates.event_date,
            lag(sev5_dates.event_date) OVER (ORDER BY sev5_dates.event_date) AS prev_date
           FROM sev5_dates
        ), sev5_sessions AS (
         SELECT sev5_gaps.event_date,
            sum(
                CASE
                    WHEN sev5_gaps.prev_date IS NULL OR (sev5_gaps.event_date - sev5_gaps.prev_date) > 7 THEN 1
                    ELSE 0
                END) OVER (ORDER BY sev5_gaps.event_date ROWS BETWEEN UNBOUNDED PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW) AS session_id
           FROM sev5_gaps
        ), session_bounds AS (
         SELECT sev5_sessions.session_id,
            min(sev5_sessions.event_date) - 3 AS session_start,
            max(sev5_sessions.event_date) + 3 AS session_end,
            min(sev5_sessions.event_date) AS first_sev5_date,
            max(sev5_sessions.event_date) AS last_sev5_date
           FROM sev5_sessions
          GROUP BY sev5_sessions.session_id
        ), brent_groups AS (
         SELECT d_1.full_date,
            op.price,
            count(op.price) OVER (ORDER BY d_1.full_date ROWS BETWEEN UNBOUNDED PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW) AS fill_group
           FROM analytics.dim_date d_1
             LEFT JOIN oil.fact_prices op ON op.date_sk = d_1.date_sk AND op.oil_type::text = 'Brent'::text
          WHERE d_1.full_date >= '2025-01-01'::date AND d_1.full_date <= CURRENT_DATE
        ), wti_groups AS (
         SELECT d_1.full_date,
            op.price,
            count(op.price) OVER (ORDER BY d_1.full_date ROWS BETWEEN UNBOUNDED PRECEDING AND CURRENT ROW) AS fill_group
           FROM analytics.dim_date d_1
             LEFT JOIN oil.fact_prices op ON op.date_sk = d_1.date_sk AND op.oil_type::text = 'WTI'::text
          WHERE d_1.full_date >= '2025-01-01'::date AND d_1.full_date <= CURRENT_DATE
        ), brent_filled AS (
         SELECT brent_groups.full_date,
            max(brent_groups.price) OVER (PARTITION BY brent_groups.fill_group) AS price
           FROM brent_groups

        CASE
            WHEN sb.session_id IS NOT NULL THEN sb.session_end - sb.session_start + 1
            ELSE NULL::integer
        END AS session_length_days,

   FROM analytics.dim_date d
